## Florence: not just art

Anyone arriving in Florence for the first time is enchanted by the Duomo, Palazzo Vecchio and the Uffizi. But the soul of the city is discovered at the table too: in Florentine cooking, made of simple dishes, honest ingredients and recipes handed down for generations.

The secret is choosing the right districts. A few minutes on foot from the centre take you to the **Oltrarno**, among craft workshops and squares where Florentines still live, or to **Santa Croce**, between the basilica and the Bargello. This is where the typical trattorias and osterias are, away from the restaurants that are too touristy.

Here is a two-day itinerary, with stops to eat along the way.

## Day 1, morning: the centre, from the Duomo to the Uffizi

Start early from **Piazza del Duomo**: the cathedral of Santa Maria del Fiore, Brunelleschi's dome, Giotto's bell tower and the Baptistery. Then walk down to **Piazza della Signoria** and **Palazzo Vecchio**, and go into the **Uffizi**, where Botticelli, Leonardo, Michelangelo and Caravaggio are waiting.

A tip: book the Uffizi and the climb to the dome a few days ahead, so you skip the queue and have more time for the rest.

## Day 1, lunch: across the Arno, on Via Romana

From the Uffizi cross the **Ponte Vecchio** and carry on along Via Guicciardini to the **Pitti Palace**. From Piazza Pitti starts **Via Romana**, the street that runs to Porta Romana alongside the Boboli Gardens. Three of our places are here, a few steps from one another. See also [where to eat near the Pitti Palace](../../where-to-eat/oltrarno-pitti-palace/).

### Trattoria Boboli 1978 Gluten Free

**Via Romana 45/R, Oltrarno.** Our historic trattoria, a short walk from the Boboli Gardens: a simple, welcoming room, informal service, time that slows down. On the table are the great classics of Tuscan cooking, such as fresh pasta from our workshop, ribollita, peposo, grilled meat and seasonal sides, with a good glass of Tuscan red. And it is all gluten free.

Why choose it:

- traditional Tuscan cooking, done properly;
- the feel of a real trattoria, with Florentines and travellers alike;
- the location, perfect for lunch between the Pitti Palace and the Boboli Gardens;
- the whole menu is 100% gluten free, so anyone can sit at the table.

### Trattoria Boboli To-Go Gluten Free

**Via Romana 52, Oltrarno.** If you are in a hurry or want to keep walking, here you will find sandwiches made with bread from our workshop, to take away. It is the right quick break between one museum and the next.

## Day 1, afternoon: Pitti Palace, Boboli and Santo Spirito

After lunch everything is close by: the collections of the **Pitti Palace**, the fountains and avenues of the **Boboli Gardens**, and then, wandering the lanes of the Oltrarno, the **Basilica of Santo Spirito**, the last church designed by Brunelleschi, with its lively square.

Before dinner, stop for a glass at **Trattoria Boboli La Cantina Gluten Free**, at **Via Romana 60/R**: a selection of Tuscan wines, pairings and tastings, for those who choose the dish starting from the glass.

[Discover Trattoria Boboli La Cantina Gluten Free](../../restaurants/la-cantina/)

## Day 2: Santa Croce, the Bargello and dinner

The second day is for Michelangelo's Florence. Start from the **Bargello Museum**, the old palace of the podestà, with sculptures by Donatello and Michelangelo. Then walk along **Via Ghibellina**, where **Casa Buonarroti** also stands, to the **Basilica of Santa Croce**, which holds the tombs of Michelangelo, Galileo and Machiavelli.

At sunset climb to **Piazzale Michelangelo** for the most famous view of the city, then come back to Via Ghibellina for dinner. See also [where to eat near Santa Croce](../../where-to-eat/santa-croce-bargello/).

### Trattoria Boboli Al Bargello Gluten Free by il Gatto e la Volpe

**Via Ghibellina 147/R, Santa Croce.** If you love osterias with a strong character, this is your place: brick walls, close tables, chatter and hearty dishes. It is the place many Florentines knew as **il Gatto e la Volpe**, now part of the Boboli family. Dinner here takes its time: straightforward cooking, no frills, ingredients in the lead, generous portions and a good wine. All gluten free.

Why choose it:

- a real Florentine osteria, a short walk from the Bargello and Santa Croce;
- traditional cooking and intense flavours;
- an informal, convivial room, ideal for dinner for two or with friends;
- the whole menu is 100% gluten free.

## What to eat during your visit

Between one museum and the next, try bistecca alla fiorentina, ribollita, chicken liver crostini and peposo at least once. We describe them one by one in the [guide to Florentine food](../florentine-food-guide-typical-dishes/), and the story of our family dishes is in [The historic recipes of Casa Boboli](../boboli-family-recipes-peposo/).

## A few practical tips

- **Book your table for dinner**, especially at weekends: the best-loved trattorias fill up early.
- **Have lunch like the Florentines**: between 12:30 and 2:30 pm the kitchen is at its best.
- **Walk**: the historic centre is small, and the loveliest districts are discovered on foot.
- **If you are coeliac**, choose places where the whole kitchen is gluten free, not just a few dishes: it is the only way to eat the typical dishes without a worry.

## Florence is loved with the palate too

Florence is loved with the eyes, but with the palate too. Between a visit and a stroll, stopping at a typical trattoria is the best way to feel truly part of the city, and to take home a memory that tastes of Tuscany.
